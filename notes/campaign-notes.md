# Campaign Notes — Source Material

**Not published.** This folder is listed in `exclude:` in `_config.yml`, so
Jekyll never builds it and it does not appear on GitHub Pages. It lives in the
repo as the single working record behind the chronicle: raw session notes,
roster data, rules text and canon decisions. If the campaign material is ever
reused for something other than prose — a wiki, a warband tracker, stat cards,
a TTS import — this file is the source to work from, not the chapters.

Chapter files reference these entries by the same keys used in their front
matter: `prolog`, `battle-1`, `battle-2`, `battle-3`.

| ref | DE title | EN title |
|---|---|---|
| `prolog` | Auf Flügeln aus Feuer | On Wings of Fire |
| `battle-1` | Das Urteil im Nebel | The Verdict in the Fog |
| `battle-2` | Die verbrannten Seiten | The Burned Pages |
| `battle-3` | Was der Juwelier bezahlte | What the Jeweller Paid |

**Title convention:** chapter titles are evocative, not merely descriptive, and
each one is echoed by a phrase inside its own chapter — "auf Flügeln aus Feuer"
from the prophecy, Styrkarr's verdict in the fog, the burned parchment of the
library, the jeweller's payment and what it really cost. Avoid titles that are
just the location.

## 2. Warband rosters

### 2.1 Marauders of Chaos — Rangvald's Reavers (Norse)

| Role | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Marauder Chieftain | **Rangvald** | Warband leader |
| Seer | **Styrkarr Skysplitter** | Mark of Tchar; spellcaster |
| Champion | **Ulfrik** ("der Pfähler" / "the Impaler") | |
| Champion | **Wulfrik** | not yet prominent |
| Henchmen | **Defiler**, **Skullsmasher**, … | Chaos Marauders |
| Henchmen | Warhounds of Chaos | the "Chaoshunde" of the chapters |

Unit descriptions as supplied:

- **Marauder Chieftain** — The brutish head of the warband. Leads his tribe in
  constant warfare and bloodshed to win the approval of their fickle gods.
- **Seer** — The Chieftain's advisor; their god tells them his will through the
  Seer's visions and dreams. Other tribesmen see the Seer's attunement to the
  gods as a great gift, to be equally respected and feared.
- **Champions** — The strongest and most battle-hardened warriors in the tribe.
  They fight constantly, waiting for the day their god judges them worthy of
  his service.
- **Chaos Marauders** — Wild uncivilised barbarians who seek battle and
  bloodshed. The majority of the chieftain's retinue.
- **Warhounds of Chaos** — Titanic, mastiff-like creatures, insanely dangerous
  in combat.

**Standing tension to exploit in prose:** the Seer is the spiritual link to the
gods and closest to them, but is *subordinate* to Rangvald, the actual leader.

### 2.2 Lizardmen — Kinder des Sotek / Children of Sotek

| Role | Model | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Skink Priest | **Yingling** | leader; snake tattoos (Sotek) |
| Saurus Totem Warrior | **Blutvater Ootl** | |
| Skink Great Crest | **Wonton** | Old Battle Wound (see battle-1) |
| Skink Great Crest | **Wantan** | |
| Henchmen | Skink Braves, Saurus Braves | |

Unit descriptions as supplied:

- **Skink Priest** — A handpicked priest renowned for reliability. His mission:
  observe the dry-skin plunderers and react only if they stray too close to a
  **hidden chamber within the ruined city**. He must judge when to attack and
  when not to. Skink Priests paint their skins with mystic symbols of their
  god — priests of Chotec wear winged serpents, **those of Sotek use snake
  tattoos**.
- **Saurus Totem Warrior** — After killing twenty foes a Saurus is accepted into
  one of three Warrior Totems: **Eagle, Jaguar or Alligator**. He must then kill
  the animal of his totem to prove worthiness. Totem Warriors are recruited by
  the Skink Priests for forays into ruined temples.
- **Skink Great Crests** — Leaders of raiding parties, skilled at scouting,
  tracking, ambushing and skirmishing. Their skin is more varied in colour than
  normal Skinks so they blend into jungle; they also wear leaves or cover
  themselves in mud. They patrol, report arriving plunderer ships, lay ambushes,
  and provide covering volleys while a hit-and-run band withdraws.
- **Skink Braves** — Fast, agile, intelligent; good at volleys of arrows and
  javelins. They lack Saurus staying power and swing between reckless audacity
  and sudden panic — short memories mean that once out of danger they regroup
  for another reckless attack.
- **Saurus Braves** — Thick scaly hides as natural armour; understand clear
  simple commands; excellent warriors and nothing else. Extremely stubborn, very
  hard to shift, ferocious — they rip at an enemy until slain.

**The guardian mission is the key motive:** they are wardens, not plunderers.
This retroactively explains their behaviour in all three battles.

## 3. Rules and lore supplied

### 3.1 The Norse (Rangvald's Reavers)

> To the north of the Old World live the Norse tribes: fierce barbarians,
> fur-clad and warlike — the very epitome of the warriors of Chaos. Their
> mountainous sea-bound land is haunted by all manner of twisted monstrous
> creatures, notably mutant Trolls and Giants, and by nameless things that live
> deep under the mountains. They are warriors at sea as well as on land —
> building longships in which they harass the southern lands and undertake
> journeys far to the west. The Norse have pale skin after the manner of men of
> the Empire. They are generally held to be especially tall and strong, and many
> have red or fair hair. Those that live the greatest distance from the Realm of
> Chaos are the least favoured of their gods — and the most likely to be seen
> openly in more civilised lands as a result.

**Special rules**

- **Reavers** — Proximity to the Empire and frequent raiding means the Norse
  excel at finding the best equipment and supplies quickly: **+1 to rarity rolls**
  when searching during the post-game sequence.
- **Pantheon** — The Norse worship a myriad of gods, spirits and ancestors.
  Because of this wide pantheon, the **Eye of the Gods** special rule's effect
  (becoming a Chaos Spawn or receiving a Mark) happens on **13+ instead of 12+**.

### 3.2 Mark of Tchar the Eagle (Styrkarr)

> Tchar the Great Eagle is the Changer of the Ways. He is the Master of the
> ever-mutating energy known as magic and his followers are skilful
> practitioners of the arcane arts.

Tchar is the name under which the northern tribes worship the god the Empire
calls **Tzeentch** — older lore, and Norse/Kurgan usage.

**Mark restriction:** per the rules Rangvald will also receive a Mark of Chaos
for outstanding performance, and it **must be the same Mark the Seer carries**.

Open thought (Rob's, worth keeping): one Mark per warband makes sense in fiction
too — the gods compete and feud, Chaos Undivided worships all as equals, but
splitting devotion between two gods while ignoring the rest is difficult or
foolish, and rarely survivable before punishment or spawnhood follows.

### 3.3 Spell — Wrath of the Great Eagle (Styrkarr's damage spell)

> **Difficulty: 9**
> The Seer calls Tchar to punish the ignorant and stupid for their delusion.
> Choose one enemy model within 12". The model is hit with a Strength equal to
> the difference of the Seer's Experience points and the model's Experience (to
> a maximum of 10). Armour saves are taken as normal. If the victim has more
> Experience points than the Seer, the latter is hit instead.

**Prose implication:** the spell is *judgement scaled to ignorance*. It hits
hardest against the green and the unproven, and rebounds onto the Seer when he
aims at someone more accomplished than himself. Describe it as punishment for
delusion, not as a generic magic missile — and treat any failure as Tchar
turning the judgement back on Styrkarr.

**Applied retroactively** in battle-1 and battle-2: the Seer "accuses" rather
than attacks; the verdict's weight is the gap in proven deeds; the rebound risk
is stated once in battle-1. Battle-2 closes the loop — the nameless, deedless
Skink who wrestles Styrkarr down is exactly the sort he had been judging all
day. Keep this framing in future battles.

## 4. Battle log

### battle-1 — Quayside

- **When:** early in the year 2000 IC · **Where:** Quayside, on the River Stir
- **Outcome:** Rangvald's Reavers win

Three-way unplanned encounter in heavy fog. Caravan spots Chaos on its left;
Lizardmen charge in from the river side. Caravan's right flank forced to attack.
Ottilie puts Great Crest Wonton out of action; she goes out of action too. Saurus
put several sell-swords down. Ulfrik forces Sir Honnung down. Sir Dieter goes
down under the Warhounds. Caravan withdraws to protect its wares. Standoff
between Lizardmen and Chaos; Styrkarr breaks it with magic, putting three
Lizardmen down. Children of Sotek withdraw.

**Post-battle**

- Died: Sir Dieter von Wurtzen; one Skink, one Saurus
- Sir Honnung: now immune to fear
- Styrkarr: +Strength advance
- **Wonton: Old Battle Wound** (roll D6 before each battle; on a 1 he misses it)
  — consequence of *this* out-of-action result

### battle-2 — The Great Library

- **When:** spring 2000 IC · **Where:** the Great Library
- **Outcome:** three-way stalemate, all withdrew

Reavers enter seeking forbidden knowledge about wyrdstone and the comet.
Children of Sotek tracked them through the sewers, rising via a well shaft.
Caravan, detouring toward the Merchants' Quarter after Quayside, blunders in.
Skinks pin Ulfrik; Saurus (Ootl) push through the far side; Styrkarr looses
magic; Warhounds tear into the Lizardmen and are spent. Rangvald downs a
Lizardman. A lone Skink rushes Styrkarr and wrestles him down. On the ruined
first-floor gallery a Chaos warrior snipes; Marcus and Julius Broun bring him
down but he stays conscious. Sir Honnung charges with five others against three
waiting Chaos warriors; several sell-swords go down. The gallery Chaos warrior
rises and leaps down onto Ottilie, putting her out of action a second time.
Robba slips into a rear reading room to search.

**Post-battle**

- Styrkarr: deep arm wound — misses next battle
- Sir Honnung: fell into a well hunting wyrdstone, swallowed contaminated water
  — misses next battle
- **Marcus promoted from Henchman to Hero**

### battle-3 — Wolf pit, wealthy quarter

- **When:** late spring 2000 IC · **Where:** manor of jeweller Balthasar Kessler
- **Outcome:** Reavers & Children of Sotek share the bounty; Caravan routed

Scenario: bounty per wolf killed, offered by Balthasar Kessler, whose abandoned
manor has been taken over by wyrdstone-mutated wolves. Absent: Wonton (Old
Battle Wound), Sir Honnung (recovering), Styrkarr (recovering). New: fresh
Warhounds for Chaos; Sir Olvir von der Krunkmark joins the Caravan.

Chaos and Lizardmen cut through the pack easily. Caravan misjudges the numbers;
Eckhardt and Eberhardt go down; howling draws more wolves into their flank and
rear. **Marcus and Julius Broun fire warning shots at the Chaos warriors**, who
had shown no hostility — this drags Chaos into fighting the Caravan too.
Yingling slips on rubble, takes a leg wound (survives better than expected due
to a Toughness advance). A **frost sabre** emerges with the last unhurt wolves
and surrounds Sir Olvir, Ottilie and Robba. Robba goes down; Sir Olvir fights on
from the ground against two wolves. A Warhound, whipped on by Ulfrik, kills the
frost sabre — earning the name **Frost-Sabre-Slayer** — then collapses. Julius
Broun's eight silver arrows fail against Rangvald's toughness. Three Chaos
warriors attack the trade wagon fruitlessly, then two turn on Broun, who kills
one with his own dagger. Lizardmen finish the pack with disciplined precision.
Caravan's withdrawal becomes a rout.

**Post-battle**

- Bounty paid out generously by Kessler
- Rangvald finds a broken, mad survivor in an outbuilding and **sacrifices him**
  over a wolf carcass — gods take notice
- Julius Broun: +WS advance
- Several Skinks: +Initiative advances
- Died: the Frost-Sabre-Slayer Warhound; Eckhardt and Eberhardt
- **Robba robbed** while unconscious — lost her goods (post-battle injury result)
- Rumour: a dark ritual might return the Warhound from the dead, at a price

## 5. Writing rules for the chronicle

- **Out of Action ≠ death.** In battle text, models only "go down" or are
  "removed from the fight". Deaths, lasting injuries and recoveries belong
  **exclusively in the Nachwirkungen / Aftermath section**, framed as what the
  post-battle rolls revealed.
- Scenario beasts (wolves, the frost sabre) may simply be killed in the battle
  text — no post-battle roll applies to them.
- Flowing prose only. No bullet points, no tables in the chapters.
- Atmospheric and detailed in a Tolkien-ish register, but not overblown:
  environment, comet damage, season, weather, what a place looks like.
- German text uses German terms, **except** established proper names and terms
  kept on purpose: Warbands, Chaoschampion, Skink-Priester, Great Crest,
  Wyrdstone, Old Ones, Slann, Skink, Saurus.
- English must read as native English — no calques, no German sentence shapes.
- Weave origins and background in only where they fit; never force them.
- **Every gain needs a reason.** Rob supplies advances as plain mechanics —
  "model X permanently gained +1 Attack", "gained Fearless", "promoted to Hero".
  Never report the mechanic as such. Give it a cause anchored in the battle just
  described, tied where possible to the character's origin, training or nature,
  and let the effect appear as a consequence at the end rather than as an
  announcement at the start. Prefer going long over going short here. If the
  session notes offer no usable trigger, invent one that fits the opponent, the
  place and the character — and ask when an important development has no anchor
  at all.
  Worked examples now in the chapters: Sir Honnung's fearlessness (he lay under
  Ulfrik's shadow waiting for a killing blow that never fell, and has never
  spoken of it); Styrkarr's Strength (Tchar reshapes the servant rather than
  paying him); Wonton's Old Battle Wound (Lustrian physiology against Ostermark
  damp); Marcus's promotion (Tilean pavise drill finally tested); Julius Broun's
  Weapon Skill (a scholar who learned what his art is worth at dagger range);
  the Skinks' Initiative (a night of reading wolf feints).
- **The outcome line is a sentence, not a label.** The `victor:` front-matter
  field renders under "Ausgang" / "Outcome" at the foot of a chapter. It must say
  what actually became of each side — who held the field, who withdrew and with
  what — never just a warband name.
