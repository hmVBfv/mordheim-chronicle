A seer who cannot point is no seer.

The skink had laid his right arm open, and of all the limbs a man can lose in a battle it had to be that one. What a seer of Tchar does, he does not do with strength but with a gesture: he turns his lord's attention onto a single point and steps aside. The hand that does the turning now hung in linen, and for the first few days he could not raise it as far as his own shoulder. Rangvald watched his seer test the fingers each morning, one after another, and said nothing about it. There is no comfort in a warband of northmen that does not count as weakness.

So the chieftain did what he could do: he filled up what could be filled. You do not buy hounds of Chaos, you gather them. In a city where nobody feeds anything, every pack sooner or later stops being a pack and becomes a number of animals that follow wherever blood falls. The Reavers spent three weeks working through the northern streets, and whatever attached itself to them along the way, they beat into an order that understood it. Two of the hounds died in the understanding. The rest, on the day it began, ran close to Ulfrik the Impaler, because he was the one carrying the whip for as long as Styrkarr's arm hung in its sling.

In the caravan a different wound weighed heavier, because it could not be looked at. After the library, Sir Honnung von Hoiser had lowered himself into one of the shafts, convinced he had seen wyrdstone down there, and had come into contact with the water that has stood in such shafts since the comet. What followed came slowly. First the taste of iron he could not rid himself of, then the fever, then days in which a man who no longer knows fear lay in his blanket and asked for a candle. The caravan's surgeon, a man out of Bögenhafen who ordinarily straightens axles and pulls teeth, told Robba after the second week that he was no use here and that the water of this city works differently in every man. It helped that Honnung was built like an ox. It did not help particularly quickly.

A replacement, meanwhile, was standing by the wagon, waiting to be spoken to. **Sir Olvir von der Krunkmark** had not come to Mordheim to look for stones. In Nordland a boy learns to dread the longships of the Norse before he ever sees one, and Olvir had heard that there were northmen loose in this city with nobody to stop them. He had nothing but the horse he had been obliged to sell on the road and a set of harness rather better than his claim to it. Robba asked him no questions about his reasons. A trader who gets a knight at a sell-sword's price, because he intends to go where the danger is anyway, does not sit long over the arithmetic.

Robba herself went back to the library twice in those weeks, alone and without announcing it. In the rear reading room, the one she had pushed her way into during the fight, there had been something she never got to — shelves the collapse had spared, and a door behind them. The first time she reached the room and found it not as she had left it. The second time she did not reach the room. There was no third time.

The Children of Sotek did not replace their dead. Nobody was hired, nothing was paid, and no one arrived from anywhere far away — nobody arrived at all. What comes out of the same pool is not resupplied; a brood that once came out of the water together only ever gets smaller after that. What happened in those weeks was nothing but closing up. Where two had been missing, the rest stood nearer to one another, and because in temper and movement they resemble each other like two impressions of the same seal, it looked from outside as though nothing had been lost. That is why the people of Mordheim believed there were always the same number of lizards. They were counting shapes, not gaps. Wonton went with them as long as he could. His leg decided the matter freshly every morning, and it did not always decide the same way.

At the end of those weeks a board was nailed to a post in the wealthy quarter, and on the board there was a number. Everyone who read it passed it on, and everyone who passed it on made it larger.
