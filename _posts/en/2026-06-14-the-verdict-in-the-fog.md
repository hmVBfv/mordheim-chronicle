### At the Quay

Quayside had once been the beating heart of Mordheim's trade — that long row of wharves and warehouses on the bank of the River Stir, where barges from half the Empire unloaded their goods. Now the jetties lay broken in the water, half-sunken barges jutting like the ribs of dead beasts from the sluggish current, and the warehouses gaped at the sky with caved-in roofs. Above it all a loading crane of black oak still stood, its arm slanted out over the water, a hook hanging from its chain that had not moved since the comet. The Stir itself flowed dark and thick, and where the shadow of a sunken hull lay across the water it glowed green far below the surface, down where the comet had carried its poison to the riverbed. Across the water the city stood as an outline in the haze — towers without roofs, too distant for detail.

It was still early in the year, and the cold lay biting over the city. Hoarfrost held only where no light reached it, on the north faces of the warehouses and under the jetties; everywhere else water stood in shallow pools between the broken stones and gave the sky back without making it any brighter. The warriors' breath stood before their faces like smoke, and off the water rolled a fog thick as wet wool — so dense that a man might lose the fellow at his side an arm's length away. Not a sound stirred but for the steady drip of the fog and the slap of the river against rotten timber. Three gulls sat close together on the bollards at the edge of the quay and did not cry out. They watched the men come, and stayed where they were.

### The Encounter

Into this fog the three bands stumbled upon one another, none looking for a fight. None had expected the others; none was willing to give ground.

The caravan was the first to mark the danger — Chaos warriors and their hounds, stepping from the murk to their left, outlines first, then shapes, then the glint of blades. Hastily they formed up to defend, their wagon and wares at their backs. In that same moment the Chaos horde began to move, gathering itself to charge. And then, out of the fog from the river, the Lizardmen came surging forward, led by their Great Crests **Wonton** and **Wantan** — swift, cold-blooded, unstoppable, their scales dark with damp.

The caravan's right flank, caught between the two advancing hosts, had no choice but to attack.

The fight was short and bloody on both sides. **Ottilie**, Robba's apprentice and anything but a trained blade, fought back in desperation against the Great Crest **Wonton** — and against all expectation wrestled him to the ground, out of action but alive — before she herself was overcome and dragged down in turn. The Sauruses of the Children of Sotek proved just as dangerous — hired mercenary blades of the caravan went down beneath their strokes, torn out of the fray. In the thick of it the Chaos champion **Ulfrik the Impaler** forced **Sir Honnung von Hoiser** to his knees, while **Sir Dieter von Wurtzen** collapsed beneath the howling Chaos hounds and did not rise again.

To protect their wares — and their lives — the Ardent Caravan withdrew from the fight, back into the sheltering fog, and left the field to the two remaining powers.

### The Standoff

What followed was a moment of icy tension. Lizardmen and Chaos warriors stood face to face, scarcely more than shadows in the white murk, each side knowing that a single wrong move might mean death. The silence of Quayside was broken only by the dripping of the fog and the deep, throaty growl of the Chaos hounds.

It was **Packmaster Styrkarr Skysplitter** who broke the deadlock. Branded upon his brow is the Mark of Tchar, the Great Eagle — the name by which the northern tribes worship the god the Empire names, with dread, Tzeentch: the Changer of the Ways, master of that restless, ever-mutating power men call magic. When a seer of Tchar calls upon him, he does not strike; he accuses. What the Great Eagle brings down upon a chosen victim is neither lightning nor fire but a verdict upon his folly, and its weight is measured by nothing except how little that victim has yet proven in life. One after another Styrkarr picked his marks out of the fog, and the three he singled out were young and untested. All the distance between their ignorance and what he had earned in years of bloodshed came down on them like an anvil. They sank to the ground before their ranks could so much as react.

Had he levelled his finger at one whose deeds outweighed his own, the verdict would have rebounded upon him instead, for Tchar draws no line between enemy and servant where presumption is concerned. A seer of the Great Eagle learns early to choose his accusations with care.

The Children of Sotek, beaten and thinned, withdrew into the fog from which they had come, and the river swallowed their trail.

**Rangvald's Reavers** remained the victors of the first battle for Mordheim.

### Aftermath

Only when the fog lifted and each band bore away its fallen to tend them did the true price of that first encounter reveal itself. Not everyone who had gone down would wake again.

**Sir Dieter von Wurtzen** had attached himself to the caravan the way landless knights do: for pay, for rations, and for the chance of winning back abroad what had been gambled away at home. The hounds reached him before he could even bring his shield up, and what the Reavers left behind was no longer a man one buries with honours — Robba had him put into the mud of the bank without arms or priest, because the air of Quayside punishes every delay with sickness. Among the Children of Sotek a Skink and a Saurus warrior did not return to life. They had no names, none at any rate that a man could have pronounced. Among the lizardmen, those who come out of the same pool come out of it together, and are known by that afterwards: by the water and by the year, not by themselves. The Skink had been one of the quick ones who run ahead and come back and think neither of the two is courage; he lay between two barrels with the blowpipe still in his hand and the dart never sent. The Saurus warrior had driven three sell-swords off the crates before a bolt took him under the jaw. He did not go down at once. He stood a while the way those creatures stand when they rest — upright, eyes open, ready at any moment — and fell only once nobody was watching. Their brothers carried them both away without a word, the whole way back into the dark beneath the city. Not out of grief, for which they have no word, but because what comes out of the water together belongs together.

Four dead, then, the City of the Damned claimed on that first day, and only one of them will ever have a name spoken over him again.

Other things grow out of blood as well. **Sir Honnung von Hoiser** had been driven to his knees beneath Ulfrik's blade — beneath the blade of a man who takes his byname from what he does to the living. What happened in the moments after, the knight has never described to anyone, not to Robba and not to his brothers-in-arms: that he lay on the wet cobbles with the Chaos champion's shadow across him, waiting for the stroke that would finish him. It never came. Whether the fog drew the champion off, whether a shout pulled him away, whether it was nothing but whim, Honnung never learned. Reikland teaches a knight a great deal about an honourable death and not one word about the minutes afterwards, when it has failed to arrive. Anyone who has watched him go into a fight since has noticed that his eyes no longer search out the way back. He has already been through the moment of his own death; whatever comes at him now can offer him nothing but that same moment a second time.

The powers **Styrkarr Skysplitter** serves had likewise heard the threefold verdict in the fog. Tchar rewards with neither gold nor land, but by reshaping the servant himself. In the weeks after Quayside the Packmaster grew broader across the shoulders, the cords of his arms standing out like rope, and he lifted without effort what he would once have strained at. His men marked it with that mixture of awe and unease the seer draws in any case — for a man who accepts gifts from the Changer of the Ways surrenders some say over his own body, and none of them knows what shape the next gift will take.

**Wonton**, whom Ottilie had wrestled down in the fog, woke again — but the wound from that fall never closed properly. The Children of Sotek come from the close heat of Lustria, and what would scar over there within days stays open and stiff in the damp cold of Ostermark. On some mornings, when the fog creeps up off the Stir, the leg will not carry him. Then the Great Crest stays behind in camp and watches his brothers set out without him.

Three warbands. One fog. One first victor. The City of the Damned had only just begun to exact its toll.
